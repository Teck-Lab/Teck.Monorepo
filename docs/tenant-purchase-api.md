# Tenant purchase and onboarding API

Billing owns an immutable, server-priced purchase quote. Customer reserves a new business tenant GUID and slug bound to the authenticated owner's signed Keycloak subject. `POST /purchases` accepts `ReservationId` (Customer's GUID), plan, and database options; it does not accept a caller-owned tenant slug or price. Billing fetches the canonical reservation with its `client_credentials` service identity. A buyer with no organization claim can quote only a reservation bound to its `sub` and may not override the beneficiary. A signed `sales` realm role may supply `BeneficiarySubjectId`; Billing verifies that subject against Customer and persists both the seller actor and owner.

`POST /purchases/{orderId}/payment-intent` is accessible only to the signed reservation owner while the order is pending. Billing uses official `Stripe.net` 53.0.0 to create one unconfirmed PaymentIntent using its saved minor-unit price/currency and immutable order/tenant metadata. The API returns its ID and client secret for a later Stripe.js client; **no frontend is implemented here**. Retries return the bound intent and cannot create a second payable order intent. Supply `Stripe__SecretKey` and `Stripe__WebhookSecret` through runtime secrets or AppHost secret parameters, never committed configuration. Only `POST /stripe/webhooks` accepts a raw `Stripe-Signature`-verified `payment_intent.succeeded`; Billing retrieves that intent again from Stripe and requires exact ID, succeeded status, amount, received amount, currency, order and tenant metadata before confirming the order and publishing the immutable event through Wolverine. Other statuses, unsigned callbacks, simulated payment rows and the development `StubPaymentProvider` do not prove capture. The owner sees progress through `GET /purchases/{reservationId}`.

Before contacting Stripe, Billing commits a durable `StripeIntentAttempted` marker and its first attempt time under the same tenant-wide purchase/purge lock. If Stripe accepts an intent but the process loses its response, the pending order remains non-purgeable and cannot be confirmed by external invoice; an owner retry uses the order's stable provider idempotency key to recover the intent ID only within 23 hours of the first attempt. Stripe may prune keys after 24 hours, so later unbound owner retries return `409 Conflict` rather than risking a second intent: operator reconciliation of the provider outcome is required, with no automatic purge. Foreign orders remain `404 Not Found`. An order with a bound Stripe intent likewise cannot also be confirmed by external invoice; the invoice route returns a conflict instead of creating two independent payment paths for one order.

Alternatively, a `platform-manager`-authorized operator can confirm an externally verified invoice with its reference and the signed verifier subject. This privileged attestation is recorded on the order. Retrying the same order with the same trimmed invoice reference and verifier returns the recorded paid order without publishing again; changing either attestation conflicts. The real RabbitMQ regression observes one broker-delivered confirmation after a valid signed Stripe webhook, none before capture, and none from a replay. It does not yet prove recovery from a failed publication transaction.

Customer correlates confirmed payment by tenant ID, canonical slug, and source order ID; mismatches are rejected. Paid tenants remain `paid_pending_readiness` and inaccessible until both licensing and database readiness. `GET /purchases/{reservationId}` returns the order only to the signed Keycloak subject stored as its beneficiary, including before a buyer has tenant membership or a `tenant_id` claim. Cancellation and renewal are separate lifecycle transitions, not payment confirmation.

`POST /purchases/renewals` creates a distinct pending annual order for the verified current paid period and owner. A captured Stripe intent or audited external invoice confirms that order against its predecessor, retains the subscription ID, and extends validity from the previous paid expiry when prepaid; a quote alone never extends access.

`POST /purchases/{orderId}/cancel` requires the signed reservation beneficiary. It cancels only an already-paid active order; a pending PaymentIntent returns `409 Conflict` rather than silently leaving a payable intent behind. The first cancellation returns the persisted order with `CancelledAtUtc` and publishes one Billing-signed suspension event; owner retries return the same state without a second event. Other subjects cannot cancel the order.

Paid orders have one year of validity. Customer persists the signed paid `ValidUntilUtc` and denies tenant database metadata immediately at that exclusive bound, even if the Billing sweep or broker is late; existing paid rows without a verified bound also fail closed. The gateway checks that bound on every successful Customer metadata lookup, including cached and fail-safe results, rather than allowing its five-minute metadata cache to extend paid access. Billing's hosted `SubscriptionExpiryService` scans at startup and every `SubscriptionExpiry:PollInterval` (one hour by default). At expiry, it marks still-active orders `suspended` once and signs a `renewal_failed` access-suspension event dated to the original validity end; Customer and Licensing consume the same proof as cancellation. The sweep does **not** collect a renewal charge or extend validity; only a separately verified provider capture or audited invoice on a pending renewal extends entitlement.

`POST /tenants/{tenantId}/purge-approvals` is an **admin-only Customer API**, not an owner or self-service route. The caller needs a real Keycloak `platform-manager` permission on `customer:purge`, and two different signed operator subjects must approve the same paid dedicated tenant **after** its configured suspension recovery window. The route accepts no order ID, price, tenant slug or approval actor in a body; Customer reads the current global registry and appends each operator's immutable audit record. The first approval returns `202`, a repeated actor or ineligible tenant returns `409`, and the second returns a Customer-signed decision and publishes it through the outbox. **Do not enable the worker's destructive purge yet**: its worker-local order check misses Billing-paid renewals not delivered over RabbitMQ, and the retained Backup CR UID does not protect backing snapshots from concurrent deletion. A durable Billing paid-order fence, independently protected snapshot retention, and disposable CNPG staging restore are required. See `docs/tenant-database-reconciler.md`.

## Billing to Customer internal verification

`GET /internal/purchase-reservations/{reservationId}` uses the Customer-issued GUID and is protected by `BillingPurchase`. It returns tenant ID, canonical slug, owner subject, status, and existing order correlation. Customer requires a signed `customer-api`-audience token with `azp=billing-api`, the Billing-client-only `billing_service_identity=billing-api` marker, and the named `tenant-purchase` scope. Configure Billing's `CustomerReservations:TokenEndpoint`, `ClientId`, `CustomerBaseUrl`, and `Scope` (`tenant-purchase customer-api-audience`); inject `CustomerReservations:ClientSecret` at runtime. Billing obtains the token via `client_credentials` before each lookup. A normal user token cannot enumerate reservations.

## Customer organization administration

Customer's paid-event consumer first persists the correlated subscription snapshot, then `KeycloakOrganizationProvisioner` verifies the reserved owner `sub`, creates or retrieves a Keycloak organization bound to the Customer tenant GUID through the `customerTenantId` attribute, and links that owner as a member. Duplicate events retry failed links without issuing a second organization; wrong GUID/alias adoption fails. The Customer registry keeps both its canonical tenant GUID and the generated Keycloak organization GUID. Keycloak **26.8.1** is required here: pre-26.7 organization administration required the broad `manage-realm` role. The `customer-org-provisioner` service account needs the tested `realm-management` roles `manage-organizations`, `view-users`, and `manage-users` (the last for adding an existing owner member); never grant `realm-admin`/`manage-realm`. Configure `KeycloakOrganizations:ServerUrl`, `Realm`, `AdminClientId`; inject `AdminClientSecret` at runtime, never commit it. Deployments must verify image version, import/reconcile the role mapping, and supply this secret before accepting paid events.

## Event handoff

The stable event payload and consumer/idempotency contract are documented in [subscription-payment-confirmed-event.md](subscription-payment-confirmed-event.md). Consumers use immutable feature/database/plan snapshots. Paid status is not a readiness signal.
